# Write Lecture Notes: exercises

Apply these standards within the scope selected in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). This reference does not initiate a separate review pass or expand the task.

## Exercises

Treat the lecture notes as a full exercise bank. Select homework problems from this bank rather than maintaining a separate competing problem set; retain worthwhile additional practice for independent study. Organize tasks by purpose: conceptual and oral questions close the subsection in Review Questions; assumption-changing extensions sit near the assumptions they examine; computational and plotting exercises follow the worked examples that supply the necessary methods. Examples provide natural guidance without separate hints that reveal the answer.

- Use the course's numbered `exercise` environment with a concise subject title, a short motivating setup, and explicitly enumerated parts for multipart problems. Define all inputs and requested outputs. Place exercises near the concepts they assess, and separate unrelated tasks rather than combining them under one title.
- Distribute graduate-level exercises immediately after the related concepts and worked examples instead of clustering them at the end of each subsection. Their placement should provide a gentle hint about which ideas or tools to use. Preserve the definition–example and result–proof–worked-example sequences. Place synthesis exercises after the concepts they connect have been developed. Cover **every key concept with at least one exercise** and include **at least three substantive problems per subsection**. Treat three as a floor, not a target: add as many as students need to apply the material in depth. Do not count review questions as substitutes for these exercises.
- Give each exercise a clear motivational context and a well-defined task. Vary the work across derivation, interpretation, computation, and evaluation of assumptions as appropriate. Include informative corner cases, pathologies, and counterexamples where they deepen understanding; do not pad the count with repetitive calculations.
- For suitable data-based exercises, use publicly available datasets accessible in R. Identify the dataset and package or source, specify the variables and needed preparation, and verify access and the properties used in the question. Use the data to reveal an interesting property or extension, rather than merely to supply numbers.
- When an exercise introduces an extension beyond the notes, first give the background and definitions needed to make it self-contained. Give a hint only when the problem is fundamentally tricky; make the hint point toward a productive idea without doing the central work.
- Include the required Review Questions box in every subsection and use it to test connections and interpretation. Mark optional extension material according to existing course conventions; this does not make any of the four required boxes optional.

### Comprehensive investigations

- Build a comprehensive bank within each substantively developed subsection: include phenomena to examine, meaningful technical development, computational experiments, and simple real datasets for exploration and visualization wherever these are relevant to its subject. Three exercises and concept coverage alone do not establish this breadth. For a purely theoretical subsection, use suitable analytic examples or counterexamples and connect empirical work to the nearest relevant application rather than inventing an artificial data task.
- Design connected multipart investigations where appropriate: begin with a phenomenon or question, develop the mathematical mechanism or prediction, examine it through a controlled computational experiment, and explore how it appears in real data. End with interpretation that relates evidence to the original question and distinguishes theoretical guarantees from empirical observations. Individual exercises may focus on one component; the bank must provide the connected breadth, rather than forcing every exercise through an identical template.
- Make computational experiments test something specific: identify the factor to vary, the comparison or baseline, the quantities to record, and the plot or summary that can reveal the phenomenon. Specify enough of the design for students to carry it out, including replication and variability when simulation is involved, while leaving the intended reasoning and discovery to them.
- Include approachable real-data investigations with a substantive question, a simple accessible dataset, clearly identified variables and units, necessary preparation, and purposeful exploratory plots. Ask students to describe visible patterns, apply or compare the relevant methods, and interpret the findings and limitations in context. Follow the dataset-access and plotting requirements in this reference; do not replace real data with simulations or add a dataset merely to supply numbers.

### Exercise motivation, alignment, and workload audit

- Begin every numbered exercise with one or two substantive sentences explaining the question or tension it investigates and its connection to a nearby concept, result, or worked example. Name what students will learn, test, or distinguish; a generic claim that an exercise is useful is not motivation.
- Audit each part against that motivation. Require outputs that actually demonstrate the intended lesson, and finish with a focused interpretation where needed. Rewrite or remove parts that do not advance it. Retain essential derivations that establish technical understanding, apply a method to a changed assumption, or expose a statistical mechanism. Reuse established lecture results; supply routine identities and intermediate updates rather than repeating proofs or matrix arithmetic.
- Audit the bank for the breadth above and trace each investigation from its motivating phenomenon through its technical or empirical work to interpretation. Repair missing computational or real-data exploration where relevant; a collection of routine derivations or disconnected plotting commands is not comprehensive.
- Minimize redundant computation: one informative plot or comparison may replace several tables, intervals, and numerical scenarios. Count the actions students must perform, not just the number of parts. Allow software for arithmetic when manual calculation is not the objective. Keep the full bank broad enough for independent study; choose homework from it rather than reducing the bank to one assignment's workload.
- Place each exercise at the first point where its prerequisites and supporting example are available, preserving definition--example and result--proof--example sequences. Record the concept-to-exercise connection during the audit and check actual source order; avoid accumulating exercises at subsection endings. Only synthesis problems should wait for several concepts. Check that an exercise does not require a theorem or algorithm taught later merely to keep it near a broad topic heading.

### Plotting-task alignment audit

When revising an example or figure, audit every dependent exercise in the same pass. Synchronize the design, sample size, distributions and degrees of freedom, plot type, panel mapping, labels, and figure references; explain intentional changes in the exercise. Preserve the exercise's distinct investigation rather than copying the example or providing its central answer. A routine plot may still cite a nearby example when that improves continuity.

Routine plots—including histograms, ECDFs, scatterplots, and other familiar displays with clearly specified variables—are exempt from mandatory example-figure references. Routine empirical-versus-theoretical CDF overlays are also exempt. More involved or complex constructions must first appear as a developed, substantive figure in a worked example in the main text. In the exercise, cite that specific figure and panel (for example, “Make a plot similar to Figure ...”), stating which axes, encodings, overlays, or interval layout to retain and what changes. Verify the actual figure and its position before the exercise; distinguish descriptive contours from calibrated credible regions. If a suitable example is missing, develop it first or revise the task. Do not substitute a schematic hint, reveal the exercise's central answer, or add unnecessary plotting work.

### Exercise style audit

Audit **every exercise** before delivery, including short practice and extension problems. Apply one consistent emphasis policy across all parts: when required action verbs are bold, bold every requested action (including those after formulas or later in a paragraph), rather than a selected subset. Keep setup, supplied facts, and mathematical quantities out of prose emphasis; let the environment style control the title. Check capitalization, punctuation, parallel phrasing, part labels, mathematical boldface for vectors and matrices, conventional error notation, defined inputs, cross-references, and explicit outputs. Separate distinct deliverables when needed without adding work. Inspect the rendered exercise for stranded setups, awkward line breaks, and ambiguous scope. Review questions remain conceptual or oral; numbered exercises form the broader bank from which homework is selected.
