# Academic precision

Use for changed mathematical or statistical claims and their dependencies; do not reopen verified, unchanged results without a concrete reason.

- Specify each claim’s objects, domain, quantifiers, assumptions, and conclusion. Check existence, finiteness, identifiability, boundary cases, and nondegeneracy where relevant. Keep sufficient conditions distinct from necessary ones.
- Track the actual estimand, estimator, and source of randomness. Distinguish population identities from valid estimation procedures; exact from asymptotic claims; pointwise from uniform results; conditional from unconditional statements; and bias from stochastic error. For limits, state the regime, scaling, and mode of convergence. For intervals or bands, identify the target and coverage event.
- Prove each substantive result under its stated assumptions, or label an honest sketch. Make essential expansions and cancellations explicit: show intermediate terms, justify remainder orders, and explain why terms vanish. Reference routine technical details by a specific earlier result or source; do not hide essential reasoning behind citations or “clearly.” Check limit/interchange arguments and identify nontrivial dependencies.
- For theoretical topics, follow the result and interpretation with a proof plan explaining each piece's purpose. Divide long arguments into motivated lemmas and propositions, each with a proof, then assemble them in a short proof of the announced theorem. Keep short arguments intact; do not repeat the theorem merely to summarize its proof.
- For central results, selectively explain where consequential assumptions enter and what fails without them. Use counterexamples, boundary cases, or alternative arguments to illuminate necessity, sharpness, and transferable proof ideas; for methods, consider identifiability, misspecification, or sensitivity where relevant. Develop the revealing questions, not a checklist for every result.
- Check algebra, dimensions, and parameterizations. Use simple special cases to check and briefly illustrate the result, explaining the exact property or limiting behavior it must recover; these checks supplement the proof. Use targeted symbolic or numerical checks only when they resolve real uncertainty. Verify reported numerical results with their stated setup; do not invent outputs.
- Verify new or changed source attributions against the actual source; retain already verified references. Cite the result or section that supports the claim, and distinguish external results from author-derived examples. Report unresolved source or mathematical uncertainty rather than silently strengthening a claim.
- Verification has separate outcomes: mathematical reasoning, source support, computational agreement, successful compilation, and visual inspection. Report only checks actually performed. Skill metadata validation establishes package structure, not the quality of generated lecture notes.

## Received review comments

- Read each substantive comment against the current source; verify the diagnosis rather than accepting the review as authority. Separate mathematical errors, missing assumptions or evidence, pedagogical weaknesses, and optional extensions.
- Keep a compact comment-to-source map with disposition and verification: addressed, already satisfied, deferred, or declined with a reason. Treat missing points as open until checked; distinguish a completed revision from a full numerical or literature audit.
- Fix consequential errors and clarity gaps within scope. Optional technical detours should not crowd out the central ideas or carefully chosen examples. If a requested change is unnecessary or would weaken the lesson, explain why rather than silently omitting it.
