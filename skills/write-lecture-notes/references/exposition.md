# Exposition

Use [precision](precision.md) for claims and proofs, [figures](figures.md) for visuals and captions, and [exercises](exercises.md) for independent practice.

## Motivation and progression

- Open with a coherent statistical tension: a relatable decision or a clean mathematical puzzle with explicit rules and target. Reveal a consequential contrast, competing explanations, or tempting shortcut. Avoid contrived applications, unrelated domain knowledge, and cleverness without purpose.
- For method development, a useful route is familiar limitation → new object → population target and observations → estimator → remaining uncertainty. A theorem, counterexample, or unifying principle may organize other topics better. Make the question and contribution clear; keep preludes brief and free of housekeeping.
- Use an instructor-supplied framework's verified terminology and relationships. Explain overlap through shared constructions; do not invent a taxonomy when the intended framework is unknown.
- Method exposition must provide an applicable procedure: inputs, assumptions, unknown-parameter estimation, data-driven tuning, fitting/refitting, and consequential failure or tie rules. Specify parameter values or how to obtain them. Follow with a comprehensive application, comparing previously learned methods on the same data when useful. Shared algorithms and protocols need one primary home.
- Keep results, interpretation, and proofs close. Separate substantial theory when it improves navigation; integrate derivations and guarantees when needed to understand a method. Introduce shared prerequisites once and honor instructor-specified ordering. For central results, use [precision](precision.md) to illuminate assumptions and proof ideas.
- Before section-wide revision, map questions, methods, investigations, explanatory results, and synthesis. Preserve agreed coverage and developed content. Each subsubsection needs a distinct role, not a uniform one-question/one-panel template. An application spanning a subsubsection should be narrative rather than one enclosing example environment.
- Give each fundamental idea a primary explanation where needed; revisit it through a new comparison, setting, or connection rather than repeated prose. State consequential limitations with the claim they qualify.
- Address likely misconceptions near the argument. Where useful, invite a prediction, comparison, or diagnosis of a plausible incorrect argument before explaining the resolution. Provide enough guidance for a reader to recover when stuck, while preserving discoveries assigned to exercises. These are selective self-checks, not mandatory classroom activities or a template for every result.
- Keep technical detail needed for reasoning or assigned work. Put sampling assumptions, conventions, grids, and preparation at first relevant use; incidental reproducibility logistics belong in source. Prelude-only edits match the existing body without redeveloping it.

## Subsection structure and readings

- Default course convention for a complete new or substantively redeveloped subsection: one **Core Reading**, **Guiding Questions**, **Review Questions**, and **Further Reading** box, using existing environments. Subsubsections contribute to parent boxes; incidental edits do not trigger new sets. Adapt placement or grouping when needed for a coherent treatment; preserve explicitly required formats.
- Usual order: setup → Core Reading → motivating prelude and useful visual → Guiding Questions → development and practice → Review Questions → Further Reading. Preserve established subsection page starts where they support layout; do not force breaks that impair readability. Use concise academic titles.
- Both question boxes use bullets, not numbering, with no fixed count. Guiding Questions express the established puzzle in accessible language, without unintroduced jargon or procedural checklists. Review Questions test connections, assumptions, interpretation, and limitations using the developed ideas. Neither replaces numbered exercises; one-to-one pairing is unnecessary.
- Verify sources and relevant chapters/sections. Core Reading prepares students; Further Reading offers optional depth or an alternative. Explain what a selected source adds and, when helpful, a route into it: a theorem, proof technique, comparison, empirical finding, or bounded extension. Connect research through substantive questions, distinguishing established results, conjectures, and proposed investigations. Do not require a common reading path or paper-review assignment. Flag unresolved references.
- Use summary boxes selectively after completed arguments: central result, decisive assumptions, and a meaningful comparison. Avoid formula inventories, automatic recaps, and box quotas; keep extended explanations in prose.

## Definitions and immediate illustrations

- Number reusable definitions when later reasoning needs their precise conditions; give objects, domains, assumptions, and the defining condition. Merge related definitions and reuse established ones. Descriptive terms, temporary quantities, and unused abstractions belong in prose; captions should not become a second home for definitions.
- An instructor-designated guided extension may define its construction in a self-contained exercise. Later extensions must recall or reference it; optional exercises cannot become prerequisites for core exposition.
- Give every new concept, definition, theorem, proposition, and lemma an adjacent elementary illustration. Use ordinary prose and stable invisible anchors, avoiding repeated “Illustration” headings or numbered example environments for routine substitutions. Small matrices and explicit resamples can make objects concrete without becoming investigations.
- Plan visual support for these illustrations as well as numbered examples; follow [figures](figures.md). One shared figure can serve several related ideas when each use identifies a distinct feature. Preserve result–interpretation–proof adjacency; illustrations never replace proof.

## Sustained investigations

- Classify by teaching purpose. A substitution or construction check is an illustration; length alone does not make an investigation. Reserve numbered examples for comprehensive investigations, and narrative exposition for applications spanning a subsubsection.
- An investigation connects a motivating question, explicit setup and assumptions, essential mathematics, evidence, and an answer with fundamental statistical insight. Reuse datasets to make comparisons meaningful. Exact analysis may supply evidence without decorative simulation; local illustrations alone cannot replace sustained method applications.
- Deepen a missing connection through reasoning or evidence, not extra headings or prose. Controlled comparisons should expose a mechanism, cost, or limitation; explain what discrepancies establish and how they inform a decision. Preserve discoveries assigned to independent exercises.
- Close by answering the opening question from the actual evidence, with consequential limitations or next questions. Avoid theorem restatements, stock “Lesson” labels, generic cautions, and repeated preceding paragraphs.
- When reclassifying short examples, preserve their content and dependent exercises, figures, and anchors. Replace obsolete “Example N” references with meaningful links. Check investigative coverage across the section, not just environment formatting.
